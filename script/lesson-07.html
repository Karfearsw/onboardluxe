Dialer Usage: Power Dialer, Dispositions & DNC

SDR · Agent 7 min read Knowledge Check (3 questions · pass 3/3)

Before You Start a Dial Session

The power dialer lives in the CRM's dialer workspace. Before your first dial of the day:

  1. Confirm your phone is set. Your assigned dialer phone number must be configured in your profile — the dialer places calls from it. If it's not set, stop and tell your manager; don't borrow someone else's line.
  2. Open your queue, highest priority first. The dialer feeds you leads in priority order — work the queue, don't cherry-pick easy names.
  3. Check the DNC flag on every lead before dialing. The dialer should surface the flag, but the final responsibility is yours. If a lead is flagged Do Not Contact, skip it — no exceptions, no "just one more try."
  4. Know your calling window. Dial only between 8:00 AM and 9:00 PM in the lead's local timezone — not yours. (Full rules in Lesson 09.)

Call Dispositions: Log Every Call

A disposition is the single code that says what happened on a call. It must be set immediately after every dial — before the next one starts.

  • Answered — You spoke to the person. Log notes + the agreed next step.
  • No Answer / Voicemail — Leave a short, professional voicemail (name, company, reason, callback number), then log it. The timestamp matters — it proves the attempt happened.
  • Callback Requested — They asked you to call back. Create a follow-up task with the specific date/time they gave — not "later."
  • Qualified — Motivation + timeline + property confirmed. Move the pipeline stage and schedule the next step.
  • Not a Fit — Log why (no motivation, wrong market, price expectations). Close the lead out cleanly.
  • Do Not Contact — They asked to be removed. Set the flag immediately, stop outreach, tell your manager.
  • Wrong Number — Mark it so nobody wastes another dial on it. Note the source if the number came from skip-tracing.
Non-Negotiable

Every dial gets a disposition. "I'll log it later" is how calls vanish from the record. Disposition first, then breathe, then dial the next lead.

DNC Rules on the Dialer

  • Opt-out = instant stop. "Take me off your list," "don't call again," "remove me" — all mean the same thing. Set the DNC flag during the call if possible, definitely within the hour.
  • National + state DNC registries are scrubbed at the list level before leads hit your queue — but scrubbing is a safety net, not a guarantee. If a person tells you they're on the Do Not Call list, treat it as an opt-out.
  • Never text a skip-traced number. Enriched numbers (Lesson 08) are manual-call-only. Cold texts to these numbers carry serious per-message liability — see Lesson 09.
  • Don't game the system. Spoofing numbers to increase answer rates, calling from personal phones to dodge flags, or re-dialing opt-outs will cost you your seat. Instantly.

Voicemail & Wrap-Up Standards

  • Voicemail under 20 seconds: your name, Ocean Luxe, why you're calling (the property), your callback number — spoken slowly. Then hang up.
  • Wrap-up time is for logging, not chatting. Set the disposition, write the notes, create the follow-up task, move on.
  • End-of-session audit: dials made, dispositions set, follow-ups scheduled, DNC flags applied. If a dial has no disposition, the session isn't done.
Key Principle

The dialer multiplies your effort — which means it multiplies sloppiness too. Clean dispositions and instant DNC flags are what let one agent safely work hundreds of dials a day.

Quick Practice

Disposition Drill

For each outcome, name the disposition AND the follow-up action: (1) You leave a voicemail. (2) They say "call me back Thursday morning." (3) They say "stop calling me."
