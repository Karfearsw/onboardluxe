CRM Workflows: Leads → Pipeline → Contracts

SDR · Agent 8 min read Knowledge Check (3 questions · pass 3/3)

The Pipeline, Stage by Stage

Every lead lives in exactly one pipeline stage at a time. The stage is not a vibe — it is a claim that a specific thing happened. Use the stage names shown in your CRM (they are being standardized company-wide); never invent your own.

  1. New — The lead just arrived and nobody has touched it yet. Your job: contact it fast.
  2. Contacted — You reached the person (or left a real voicemail / got a response). Log the attempt and set the next step.
  3. Qualified — They have a property, a motivation signal, and a timeline. Motivation + timeline + property = qualification.
  4. Appointment — A property walkthrough or detailed call is scheduled. Date and time are set.
  5. Negotiation — You know the numbers (ARV, repairs, MAO from Lesson 04) and you are making or discussing an offer.
  6. Under Contract — A purchase agreement is signed by the seller. The deal is real now — it gets treated like it.
  7. Closed — The assignment or sale funded. Only evidence-backed closes count — see Section 3.
Exit Lanes

Lost (not motivated, not a fit, unresponsive after follow-up) and Do Not Contact (opt-out) are terminal stages. Close leads out cleanly — a bloated pipeline is how real deals get missed.

Stage Discipline: What Moves a Lead Forward

A stage moves only when the logged event that earned it exists in the CRM. If it isn't logged, it didn't happen.

  • Log the earning event first, move the stage second. The call log, appointment, or signed offer is the proof; the stage is just the label.
  • One lead, one stage, one owner. Never park a lead in two stages, and never let a lead sit ownerless.
  • No stale leads. A lead untouched for more than a few days is a lead going cold. Every active lead has a next step with a date — that's what keeps them moving.
  • Never move a lead backward without a reason. If a deal falls apart, log why it fell apart, then move it to the correct stage (often back to Negotiation or Qualified) with a new next step.
  • DNC flags follow the lead forever. If a lead opted out, the flag stays no matter what stage they're in. There is no "re-activating" an opt-out.

From Negotiation to Signed Contract

The jump from Negotiation to Under Contract is where money enters the picture — so the evidence bar goes up.

Evidence-Based Close Gates
1. Signed purchase agreement on file (seller signature + date)
2. Correct property details match the lead record
3. Earnest money / deposit terms recorded
4. Title or closing coordination started
  • A deal closes in the CRM only when the evidence exists. Marking a deal closed without a signed agreement and supporting records is a reportable offense — it falsifies company revenue reporting.
  • Production data is real people only. Never create test leads, test deals, or test contracts in the live CRM. Training and testing happen in the sandbox or training environment only.
  • Contracts live in the Contracts module, linked to the lead record — not in chat threads, not in your personal drive, not in email attachments.

Your Daily Pipeline Routine

  1. Start with overdue follow-ups. Open your task queue first — anything due today or overdue gets touched before anything else.
  2. Work new leads first, then hot deals. New leads age the fastest. Then work Negotiation and Appointment leads — deals closest to money.
  3. End of day: zero orphans. Before you stop, every lead you touched has a next step with a date. Nothing sits "in limbo" overnight.
Key Principle

The pipeline is a promise to the future. Every stage move, every log, every follow-up is you telling tomorrow's teammate (or tomorrow's you) exactly what to do next.
