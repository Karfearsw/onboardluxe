The Disposition Process: Assignments & Buyers

Agent 8 min read Knowledge Check (3 questions · pass 3/3)

How Wholesaling Assignments Work

Disposition is the sell side of wholesaling: we find a buyer for the contract we hold with the seller. We don't sell the house — we sell our position in the purchase agreement via an assignment.

  1. We have a signed contract. Disposition starts only after the purchase agreement is executed (Under Contract stage). No contract, no marketing — marketing a property you don't control is a compliance violation in every state we work.
  2. We match to buyers. The CRM buyer list is filtered by market, price range, and property type to find investors who actually buy what we have. VIP/active buyers get first look.
  3. Offers are tracked. Every buyer offer on a deal is logged in the CRM — amount, buyer, date. Competing offers are compared on price, certainty (proof of funds), and timeline.
  4. Assignment agreement executed. The buyer signs the assignment; our fee (the spread between seller price and buyer price) is documented.
  5. Close. Title or the closing attorney coordinates; the deal funds and the lead moves to Closed with all evidence on file.

Buyer Matching: Quality Over Broadcast

  • Match before you blast. Filter the buyer list by market (MI, MA, RI, FL), buy box (price range, property type), and activity status. A targeted send to 10 real buyers beats a blast to 200 cold ones.
  • Warm buyers first. Buyers with a documented conversation (buy criteria, past offers) get contacted before anyone else.
  • Keep the list clean. If a buyer is unresponsive, not a fit, or a test record, flag it — don't keep blasting them. Test records don't belong in production buyer lists (real people only).
  • Log every offer. Buyer name, amount, proof-of-funds status, and date. An offer you don't log is an offer that doesn't exist when you compare.

Compliant Broadcasts

Property broadcasts (email/text blasts to the buyer list) are regulated. Every broadcast must pass this checklist:

  • Signed contract in hand. Never broadcast a property without an executed purchase agreement. "Under contract soon" is not enough.
  • Truthful representation. Represent the property as it is — we market our contract position, not a guaranteed flip profit. No inflated ARV claims, no hidden condition issues we know about.
  • Clear pricing. State the assignment price and fee structure plainly. No bait-and-switch pricing.
  • Opt-out honored. Every broadcast channel has an opt-out mechanism; buyer opt-outs are honored exactly like consumer opt-outs (Lesson 09).
  • No blind listings on public sites without company approval and state-law compliance — some states treat public advertising of wholesaled deals as unlicensed brokerage activity.
Compliance Note

Wholesaling rules vary by state — some states restrict assignment advertising or require specific disclosures. This lesson is training, not legal advice; your manager confirms the current state rules before any public broadcast.

From Accepted Offer to Funded Deal

  1. Deposit terms recorded. Earnest money or assignment deposit amounts, due dates, and who holds the funds are logged in the CRM — not in chat.
  2. Title/closing coordination started. Open with the title company or closing attorney; confirm the closing date and what's needed from the seller.
  3. Seller communication stays warm. The agent keeps the seller informed through closing — no disappearing acts after the contract is signed.
  4. Evidence on file, then Closed. Signed assignment, HUD/closing statement, and funding confirmation are the evidence gates (Lesson 06). Only then does the deal move to Closed.
Key Principle

Disposition is where trust compounds. Buyers who get clean deals with honest numbers come back. One misrepresented property burns a buyer list it took months to build.
